6 Roofing Tips Every Homeowner Should Know

Inspect Your Roof After Every Major Storm

After any storm with high winds or heavy rain, do a visual inspection from the ground. Look for missing shingles, debris on the roof, or water stains on your ceilings indoors. Catching damage early prevents small problems from becoming expensive repairs.

Keep Your Gutters Clean

Clogged gutters cause water to back up and sit against your roof line and fascia. This leads to rot, leaks, and ice damage. Clean your gutters at least twice a year — more often if you have overhanging trees — and make sure downspouts direct water away from your foundation.

Don’t Ignore Small Leaks

A small water stain on your ceiling may look minor, but by the time you see it indoors, moisture has likely been building up in your attic or walls for some time. Act quickly — water damage compounds fast and mold can begin to form within 24–48 hours of moisture intrusion.

Trim Overhanging Trees

Tree limbs hanging over your roof are a storm risk. A single fallen branch can punch through shingles or even cause structural damage. Limbs that rest on the roof also trap moisture and accelerate shingle deterioration. Keep branches trimmed back at least 10 feet from your roof.

Ensure Proper Attic Ventilation

Poor attic ventilation traps heat and moisture, which shortens the lifespan of your roof dramatically. In summer, trapped heat causes shingles to blister and crack. In winter, heat escaping from a poorly ventilated attic causes ice dams. Make sure your attic has adequate intake and exhaust vents.

Know When to Replace, Not Repair

Asphalt shingle roofs typically last 20–30 years. If your roof is aging, granules are appearing in gutters, or you’re seeing widespread curling or cracking, repairs may only delay the inevitable. Getting a free inspection helps you make an informed decision before a major failure occurs.
